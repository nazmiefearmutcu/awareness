Central bank holds rates as inflation cools

By Jane Analyst

The central bank kept its benchmark interest rate unchanged on Friday, as officials said recent data showed inflation moving steadily toward the two percent target. Policymakers voted unanimously to hold the rate steady after a two-day meeting in which they reviewed fresh employment and price figures.

In a statement released after the decision, the committee said economic activity had continued to expand at a moderate pace and that the labour market remained solid. Officials stressed that they would keep monitoring incoming information and would adjust policy if the outlook changed materially.

Markets reacted calmly. Government bond yields edged lower across the maturity curve, while equity futures were little changed. Traders now expect the first reduction in borrowing costs to come in the second quarter of next year, according to pricing in short-term interest rate contracts.

The chair of the committee told reporters that the bank had made good progress on inflation but that services prices were still rising faster than the target. She said the committee wanted greater confidence that price pressures were fading before easing policy, and she declined to give a timetable.

Economists surveyed before the announcement had largely expected no change. Several noted that the decision was consistent with the forward guidance issued in the previous quarter, which emphasised patience and data dependence rather than a predetermined path for the policy rate.

The next scheduled decision is set for the final week of the year, when the bank will also publish updated staff projections for growth, unemployment and inflation over the coming three years.
